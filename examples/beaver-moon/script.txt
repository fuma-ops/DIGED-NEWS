Look up tonight. The moon will look bigger and brighter than usual.
It's the Beaver Moon, and this year, it's a supermoon.
Why bigger? Tonight, the moon is close to Earth, about two hundred twenty-four thousand miles away. So it looks larger and brighter than a normal full moon.
Why "Beaver"? Late fall is when beavers finish their homes, before the ground freezes. The name comes from Native American and early colonial tradition.
How to see it? No telescope needed. Tonight, look east just after sunset, as the moon rises. And look for a tiny cluster of stars right next to it: the Pleiades.
Missed it? Don't worry. It's the first of three supermoons in a row. December and January are next.
